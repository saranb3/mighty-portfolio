# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

PM recruiters and hiring managers screening for APM / new-grad product roles. They arrive from a resume or LinkedIn, skim fast, and are pattern-matching for impact, judgment, and initiative. They've seen hundreds of student portfolios this cycle; the site has seconds to earn a longer read.

## Product Purpose

Personal portfolio for Saran "Mighty" Burapachaisri — rising senior in CS & Statistics at UIUC, currently PM @ Zebra, previously at Bangkok Bank and AirEstate. The site exists to convert recruiter attention into outreach. Success is twofold: they email him, and a week later they can still describe him in one line. Conversion and memorability together — not one or the other.

## Positioning

**The PM who can build it.** A product thinker with real engineering hands — user interviews and reframed briefs backed by production code, real-time ML systems, and things he founded himself. Every page reinforces that the product judgment and the technical depth live in the same person.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: **email me**. Secondary fallback: **download resume** — they leave with the PDF even if they never write.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: **"The PM who can build it."**
- Belief ladder (in order): 1) *He starts and leads things without being asked* — initiative is the hook. 2) *He's done real product work at real companies.* 3) *He has the engineering depth to back it up.* 4) *He'd be genuinely great to work with.*
- Proof on hand (inline in `src/content/projects.ts` and the `/work/*` case studies): "Most Popular" win at the Bangkok Bank internship hackathon; ScribeAR's +22% accuracy lift and ~140ms P95 latency shipped to a live research lab; GoBabyGo grown from one Bangkok chapter to 7 chapters, 200+ volunteers, 30+ cars donated; ThaiSA presidency running 12 events for a 100+ member org.

## Brand Personality

Warm, playful, bold. The voice of someone you'd actually want on your team: friendly and a little whimsical ("Hello, I'm Mighty!"), but with the confidence of shipped work behind every claim. Boldness and creativity are part of the pitch — a memorable site is itself evidence of product sense.

## Anti-references

- **Corporate consulting deck.** Buzzwords, metric soup with no soul, stock-photo professionalism.
- **Dark-mode dev terminal.** Monospace-everything, matrix-green, developer-costume aesthetics. He's technical, but the brand is a product person, not a terminal.

## Design Principles

1. **Initiative is the hook.** Lead with the things he started — founded, launched, ran — before the credentials. The work proves he can execute; the starting proves he doesn't wait.
2. **Memorable beats safe.** Success includes being describable a week later. A distinctive, personality-forward site is itself proof of product taste; template-safe design fails the brief.
3. **Substance in ten seconds.** Recruiters skim. The positioning line, the strongest proof, and the email CTA must all land without scrolling effort; depth (case studies) is one click away, never buried under style.
4. **Warmth with receipts.** Playfulness never floats free of evidence — every charming moment sits next to a concrete outcome. Charm gets the read; numbers get the email.
5. **Nothing is sacred.** The current visual identity (paper grain, rust, script hero) is up for grabs; distinctiveness and memorability win over continuity when they conflict.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA baseline: ≥4.5:1 text contrast, full keyboard navigability, and `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives for all animation. A public site that any recruiter can use, on any device.
